### 1. International Regulatory Frameworks

1. **IMO Polar Code (2016)**  
   International Maritime Organization. *International Code for Ships Operating in Polar Waters (Polar Code)*. Resolution MSC.385(94) / MEPC.264(68), London, UK.  
   URL: [https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Safety/Pages/Polar-Code.aspx](https://www.imo.org/en/OurWork/Safety/Pages/Polar-Code.aspx)

2. **IMO POLARIS Methodology (2016)**  
   International Maritime Organization. *Guidance on Methodologies for Assessing Operational Capabilities and Limitations in Ice: The Polar Operational Limit Assessment Risk Indexing System (POLARIS)*. Circular MSC.1/Circ.1519, 6 June 2016, London, UK.

3. **IEC 61174 Maritime Navigation Standards**  
   International Electrotechnical Commission. *IEC 61174: Maritime navigation and radiocommunication equipment and systems – Electronic chart display and information system (ECDIS) – Operational and performance requirements, methods of testing and required test results (Edition 4.0)*. Geneva, Switzerland, 2015.

### 3. Ice Resistance & Naval Hydrodynamics

8. **Lindqvist Ice Resistance Model (1989)**  
   Lindqvist, G. (1989). *A straightforward method for calculation of ice resistance of ships*. Proceedings of the 10th International Conference on Port and Ocean Engineering under Arctic Conditions (POAC '89), Luleå, Sweden, Vol. 2, pp. 722–735.  
   *Formulation:* $R_{ice} = R_{crushing} + R_{breaking} + R_{submersion}$.

9. **Riska et al. Ice Performance Formulation (1997)**  
   Riska, K., Wilhelmson, M., Englund, K., & Leiviskä, T. (1997). *Performance of Merchant Vessels in Ice in the Baltic*. Research Report No. 52, Winter Navigation Research Board, Helsinki, Finland.

10. **Holtrop & Mennen Open-Water Resistance (1982)**  
    Holtrop, J., & Mennen, G. G. J. (1982). *An approximate power prediction method*. International Shipbuilding Progress, 29(335), 166–170.
